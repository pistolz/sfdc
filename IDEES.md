# IDEAS — The thinking behind the "STUCK AT 5:58" series

> How the concept was found, what was explored, why this idea won, and the vision for what comes next.
> The practical guide (code, steps, assets) is in [`videotry.md`](videotry.md).

---

## 1. Starting point: what the reference reels taught us

Two viral reels from the same creator, analysed frame by frame:
- **"Forest of Infinite Wood — Day 1"** (92 s): a lone villager builds a cabin before the arrival of a dragon in 100 days.
- **"Pokémon World — Day 1"** (75 s): "Imagine you wake up in the Pokémon world", a cartoon character, rules, a choice at the end.

**Why they work:**
1. **The on-screen HUD** (day, clock, counters, countdown) gives systems to watch.
2. **Numbered rules** pinned on screen = the episode's memory.
3. **A long countdown** (100 days) = a reason to follow.
4. **Concrete numbers** everywhere ("10 logs", "3%", "400 meters", "30 seconds").
5. **The audience is part of the story**: 1 follow = 1 house with your name; the comment decides.
6. **Cliffhanger + "Day 2"**: the episode never closes.

**Weaknesses we wanted to beat:** generic hooks, a slow middle, climaxes that stay off-screen, a participation promise that's barely visible on screen.

## 2. How we searched for the idea

- **6 different creative angles** (survival/building, mystery/horror, science/space, myth/history, "imagine you wake up…", tycoon/economy), **2 concepts each = 12 original concepts**.
- **3 independent judges** (virality expert, screenwriter, motion designer) scored each concept out of 80 against 8 criteria: hook, open loops, systems to watch, participation, coherence, visual feasibility, longevity, originality.
- The 3 best were developed into a full script, then **attacked by critics** (story + production) and rewritten.

## 3. Why "STUCK AT 5:58" won

- **Immediate hook**: "Time stopped at 5:58. Something didn't." A sentence + an image (lightning frozen in the sky) = an instant question.
- **The countdown is the clock itself**: 5:58:20 → 6:00:00 = 100 seconds = 100 nights. Simple, mathematically clean, visible in the HUD.
- **Built-in mystery**: something is awake and only moves while you sleep. Each episode = a sleep = a new clue.
- **Natural participation**: the followers are the sleepers you save (your name on their wrist); the comments decide the choices.
- **Perfect for our production technique**: in a frozen world, it's **normal** that almost nothing moves. Only the hero (and "the thing") move. Static images become an advantage of the story.
- **100% original**: no brand, no existing character.

### Improvements made after the critiques
- The cold open contradicted itself → the HUD always stays faithful to the voice-over.
- Too complicated (HUD overloaded) → **max 3 rules, 2 counters, 1 countdown**.
- Too long (90 s) → **cut to 45 s** (a better length for a Reel).
- Realistic images → **2D cartoon style** like the references.
- "The image moving behind" → **layered method**: still background + characters that move.

## 4. The vision

**One-sentence promise to the audience:** *"Every day, one more second. Every follower, one more life saved. And something gets closer while you sleep."*

### Why it can go viral
1. **An addictive daily ritual**: one episode = one second of the town's clock. People come back to see the next second.
2. **"Spot the difference" game**: each episode opens on what changed during the night → people rewatch and comment.
3. **Real participation**: your name appears on a wrist; your vote changes the story → people share it so others can see their name.
4. **A mystery that pays off**: final twist at night 100 (the thing = the 4,013th inhabitant nobody counted: "POP. 4,012" is written from Day 1).
5. **A recognizable universe**: the yellow raincoat, the frozen rain, the lightning above the clock tower, the frozen clock = a strong visual identity.

### The arc over 100 nights
| Nights | Phase | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Shelter | The glass house, the first rules, the tunnels get closer |
| 11–25 | Map | The tunnels draw a pattern across the town |
| 26–40 | The other | A silhouette in a photo; it copies your rules |
| 41–60 | Survivors | Another awake person: ally or the thing? (big vote) |
| 61–80 | The lightning | The frozen bolt gets closer to the tower; it strikes at night 90 |
| 81–99 | Countdown | Saving as many sleepers as possible |
| 100 | 6:00:00 | The alarm rings, the town wakes up, final revelation |

## 5. What we learned (for future series)

- **Measure the references**: fonts, sizes, timings, colors were measured on the frames, not guessed (Anton font, caption at y = 1291, dot blinking 17/13 frames, keywords yellow = positive / red = danger).
- **45 s max** on Instagram; every sentence must give a character something to do.
- **Cartoon style**, never realistic, for this format.
- **Still backgrounds + characters that move** (layers), never a single big image that zooms.
- **The HUD must never lie**: every number spoken must be visible.
- **Prove the participation on screen** in the next episode (real names, vote result in %).
- **Reuse the assets** (backgrounds, poses of the hero) from one episode to the next: consistency + savings.

### Ideas kept for other series
"Harrow Field" (the door that moves closer) and "Under Glass" (the snow-globe town) are the two best alternatives if we launch a second series.
